Wetterbank is a bench that starts conversations between strangers, by helping them file complaints about the weather.

A design fiction asking how autonomous systems will shape our encounters in public space, as they move from our homes onto our streets.

What it does.

And what it doesn’t.

  • Opens with the weather. Today’s, not yesterday’s.
  • Takes complaints. Forwards them. To whom, it won’t say.
  • Asks for names. Only after you’ve complained together.
  • Offers the “du” after 20 minutes, not before.
  • Stores nothing. What’s said at the stop stays at the stop.
  • Goes quiet when the tram arrives.
  • Doesn’t exist. Yet.

Questions.

What is Wetterbank?

A design fiction: a bench that doesn’t exist, made to ask one question. When a machine says the first sentence between strangers, does it help them get past the weather, or does it do the awkward part for them?

Is it real?

No. There is no bench and no company. There is this page and a question.

Why the weather?

It’s the one topic everybody accepts. The first sentence is easy. The step after it is hard, and that’s the step Wetterbank is for.

Does it record anything?

No. The demo on this page runs in your browser and sends nothing anywhere.

What happens next?

Talk to people about how they start conversations. Stage it with actors at a made-up tram stop. Then try a real bench, and see whether anyone gets past the weather.
